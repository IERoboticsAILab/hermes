    H.E.R.M.E.S
    Human Enhanced Robotic Management for Emergent Swarms
    Turning human motion into swarm intent
    Saleh Abd-Elrahman  ·  2026
    The Problem

    The operator's attention doesn't scale — the robots do

    👤 Operator
    ↓
    individual commands
    🤖Robot 1
    🤖Robot 2
    🤖Robot 3
    🤖Robot 4
    🤖Robot 5
    🤖Robot 6
    🤖Robot N
    ⚠ overload
    operator is the bottleneck
    1 brain÷N robots=cognitive overload
    • Working memory caps at ~4 items. Past that, the operator drops state on individual robots.
    • Task-switching is expensive. Every gaze shift between robots costs ~0.5–1 s and accumulates errors.
    • No group-level abstraction in joystick interfaces — commands are issued one robot at a time.
    • Feedback monopolises vision — eyes are already maxed out on monitoring, no spare channel left.
    "What if commanding a swarm
    was as natural as pointing your hand
    — like a conductor?"
    Research Question
    How can wearable gestures and motion be used to control a multi-robot swarm in a way that is fast, selective, and understandable to the operator?

    APPENDIX · A1
    Gesture classifier

    Rule-based classifier — flex + IMU fusion

    DECISION ORDER

    if all(flex > OPEN_TH):       OPEN_HAND  → DRIVE
elif index_flex < POINT_TH:  POINT      → SELECT
elif all(flex < FIST_TH):    FIST       → FORMATION
elif imu_swipe_detected():    SWIPE      → PARAMS
else:                         IDLE

    Thresholds hand-tuned per operator (calibration step). IMU orientation seeds DRIVE heading only when OPEN_HAND is held for > 200 ms.

    WHY RULE-BASED, NOT LEARNED

    Latencydeterministic < 5 ms
    Training datanone required
    Failure modesexplainable
    Class count4 (small set)
    Calibrationper-operator

    A learned classifier is in Future Work — needed only when the gesture set grows or inter-operator variance matters.

    APPENDIX · A2
    Latency breakdown

    Where the 78 ms goes

    STAGE TIMINGS (mean, draft estimates)

    Sensor read & pack (glove ESP32)6 ms
    ESP-NOW transmit (glove → vest)3 ms
    Vest receive & serial frame4 ms
    USB serial (vest → Pi, 921600)2 ms
    Gesture classifier window40 ms
    Swarm intent publish (DDS)5 ms
    Robot subscribe + execute18 ms
    Total (end-to-end)~78 ms

    BOTTLENECK

    The classifier window (40 ms) dominates. Flex sensors are noisy enough that a single sample classifies unreliably — the moving-average is what gives 92% recognition.

    REDUCTION PATHS

    Kalman-filtered flex (target < 20 ms window)
    Hardware low-pass on flex line
    Predictive classifier with confidence threshold

    APPENDIX · A3
    Deadman state machine

    How the operator cuts the loop

    STATES

    ARMEDbutton held + samples fresh
    HOLDstop in place, motors at 0
    SAFEbrake + alert haptic ON

    TRANSITIONS

    ARMED → HOLD: button released OR glove silent > 200 ms
    ARMED → SAFE: classifier error spike OR loss-of-link
    HOLD → ARMED: button re-pressed AND fresh samples

    RESPONSE TIME

    Worst case ARMED → HOLD measured at 48 ± 5 ms end-to-end (button → motor stop). Safer than human reflex (~250 ms).

    FAIL-SAFE

    If the vest stops receiving glove packets, `vest_serial_bridge_node` publishes an empty sample (deadman off). The swarm sees no fresh intent → HOLD. No software intervention required.

    APPENDIX · A4
    ESP-NOW reliability

    Packet loss under realistic conditions

    LOSS BY DISTANCE (DRAFT)

    1 m, line-of-sight< 0.1 %
    3 m, line-of-sight0.4 %
    3 m, body-blocked1.8 %
    5 m, Wi-Fi co-channel3.2 %
    10 mlink drops intermittently

    WHY ESP-NOW, NOT WI-FI

    ESP-NOW is connectionless — no association overhead — so latency is bounded and packet jitter is much smaller than Wi-Fi.

    MITIGATIONS USED

    All right-glove FSR pins routed to ADC1 (avoids the ADC2/Wi-Fi conflict).
    Fixed channel (1) across all peers.
    MAC peering during bring-up — no scan loop at runtime.

    APPENDIX · A5
    Slot-auction formation

    Distributed slot assignment

    ALGORITHM (PER-ROUND)

    for each open slot s:
  bid = distance(my_pose, slot_pose[s])
  publish (my_id, s, bid) on /slot_bids
wait for all bids:
  if my bid is min for some slot s*:
    claim s*; mark s* as taken

    Convergence: O(N) rounds for N robots; each round is one ROS 2 message exchange.

    WHY DISTRIBUTED, NOT CENTRAL

    Single point of failureeliminated
    Bandwidth at the PiO(1) per round
    Adds & dropshandled naturally
    Comm. overheadO(N²) messages

    Trade-off: O(N²) message volume bounds the practical swarm size. See Future Work · scale past 4 robots.

    APPENDIX · A6
    Middleware choice

    Why ROS 2 over ROS 1 or a custom stack

    WHAT ROS 2 GAVE US

    No central masterresilience
    DDS QoS per topicreliability tuning
    Multi-host discoveryPi + 4 robots auto-mesh
    Deterministic executorslatency budget honoured
    Standard message typesreuse of OptiTrack drivers

    WHY NOT ROS 1

    Single roscore is a SPOF. Slot-auction would have to route through it. Wireless link to the Pi becomes a critical path.

    WHY NOT A CUSTOM STACK

    Re-implements 10 years of robotics middleware. No reuse of natnet drivers, no community-vetted QoS, no transport abstraction. Cost was not justified.

    APPENDIX · A7
    Communication Topology

    How the data flows

    • ESP-NOW (2.4 GHz) — gloves to vest, peer-to-peer, no router needed
    • USB serial — vest to Pi, 921600 baud, framed binary
    • ROS 2 DDS over Wi-Fi — Pi to robots, publish/subscribe
    • OptiTrack NatNet — per-robot pose, multicast
    • Haptic feedback frames travel back through the same path
